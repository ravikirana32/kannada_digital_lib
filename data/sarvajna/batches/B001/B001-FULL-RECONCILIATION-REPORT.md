## Executive result

- source-specific numbering,
- source transcription,
- cross-source correspondence,

## What is established

### 1. Source baseline

All 75 candidates have a recorded Wikisource source reading in the B001 package.

### 2. Section boundary

The source sequence changes section at item 61:

- 1–60: `ಜಾತಿಸ್ಮರಣ ಪದ್ದತಿ`
- 61 onward: `ಲಿಂಗಾತಿಶಯ ಪದ್ಧತಿ`

The section is retained as source metadata rather than discarded.

### 3. Numbering

The project does **not** assume Wikisource number N equals canonical Sarvajna number N.

The alternate online collection examined during research starts with a substantially different opening sequence. Therefore the first four are explicitly marked as a numbering/correspondence discrepancy; the remaining mappings are `unresolved` until page-level independent text is collected.

### 4. Similarity anomalies

The reconciliation engine searches for exact and near-duplicate candidates inside the acquired B001 source.

The most important known case is the pair around source items 46–47, which are extremely similar but not textually identical. They remain separate source records pending historical comparison.

## Canonical status

**0 / 75 canonical approved**

This is intentional.

A record becomes canonical only after:
1. correspondence,
2. historical/critical verification,
3. variant assessment,
4. authenticity assessment,
5. rights/provenance review,
6. editorial approval.

## Why we are not filling the unresolved mappings

The project's goal is a reliable digital edition, not simply a large transcription.

Historical sources show that Uttangi's 1924 collection contained 1,928 triplets and that a revised edition reached about 2,100; later critical work reportedly accepted a much smaller authenticated corpus and documented variants/problems in the Uttangi edition. This makes source reconciliation essential rather than optional.
